[Segment 1: California Accuses Amazon of Price Fixing in Legal Filing]

Host:  
We’re starting with some heavy news out of California, where state regulators have filed a legal complaint against Amazon. According to the New York Times Technology section, California is accusing Amazon of pressuring major brands — including Levi’s and Hanes — to encourage their competitors to raise prices on specific products. This allegation essentially points to price fixing.

The details suggest Amazon may have leveraged its dominant marketplace position to influence brands to push for higher retail prices across the board, which would be an antitrust violation. This is a significant move because it challenges the narrative of Amazon as a champion of low prices and consumer choice.

If proven, it could have ripple effects on ecommerce pricing strategies and how brands negotiate their relationships with Amazon and other platforms. Given Amazon’s massive influence on the retail ecosystem, this lawsuit is one to watch — not just for legal reasons but for its potential impact on pricing power in the luxury and mass market alike.

[Segment 2: Amazon’s Seller Count Falls as Revenue Concentrates Among Top Sellers]

Host:  
Modern Retail recently highlighted a notable trend: the number of active sellers on Amazon’s marketplace has declined from 584,000 in January 2025 to around 500,000 by March 2026. That’s a drop of roughly 14%.

Marketplace Pulse, the data source, points out that revenue is increasingly concentrated among top sellers. This suggests a shakeout where smaller or less competitive sellers are exiting or being pushed out, while the biggest players consolidate their share.

For luxury and jewellery brands, this concentration could mean fewer, but more powerful, partnerships within Amazon’s ecosystem. It also raises interesting questions about competition and diversity on the platform — key concerns amid ongoing antitrust scrutiny.

Smaller brands or new entrants might find it harder to gain visibility, while top sellers leverage scale and data to dominate. This dynamic ties back to the earlier discussion on pricing power and marketplace control, painting a picture of a marketplace that’s becoming less open and more concentrated.

[Segment 3: Pine Labs Acquires Ecommerce Enabler Shopflo for Rs 88 Crore]

Host:  
In India, Pine Labs — a leading digital payments company — has made a strategic acquisition of Shopflo Technologies for Rs 88 crore, roughly $11 million USD. This deal aims to boost Pine Labs’ ecommerce merchant services, particularly targeting mid-market and small business segments.

Shopflo specializes in enabling ecommerce operations, so this acquisition strengthens Pine Labs’ position against rivals like Razorpay and PayU. It also signals a broader trend where payments companies are integrating ecommerce enablement tools to offer more comprehensive solutions to merchants.

For luxury and jewellery brands looking to expand their online presence in emerging markets, these developments could provide more robust infrastructure and support, helping to tap into growing digital consumers.

This move highlights how digital payments and ecommerce tech are converging, enabling smoother omnichannel experiences — a must-have for luxury brands aiming to blend online and offline retail.

[Segment 4: Wholesale Lab-Grown Diamond Prices Down 14%]

Host:  
Professional Jeweller reports that wholesale prices for lab-grown diamonds fell by 14% overall in Q1 2026. While that’s a significant drop, it’s actually a slowdown compared to the more than 40% declines seen in Q1 2025.

Notably, prices for 3-carat round lab-grown diamonds fell 28% year-over-year. This data suggests the lab-grown diamond market might be reaching a new equilibrium after a period of rapid price erosion.

For jewellers and luxury brands, this stabilization is a key signal. It may encourage more investment and inventory building around lab-grown stones, which offer a more affordable and ethically appealing alternative to natural diamonds.

This trend also influences consumer perception and marketing strategies, as brands balance the allure of natural diamonds with the growing acceptance of lab-grown options in luxury jewellery.

[Segment 7: As Brands Get Tariff Refunds, What Do They Owe Their Customers?]

Host:  
Business of Fashion reports that as brands start applying for tariff refunds, they encounter a thorny issue: what about customers who paid higher prices due to tariffs?

Some consumers have launched lawsuits seeking compensation, arguing that retailers should pass savings back to them.

This raises important questions about brand responsibility and transparency, especially in a luxury context where customers expect fairness and trust.

For brands, the path forward involves legal, ethical, and PR considerations. Will refunds be shared, or will brands retain the savings? How will this affect customer loyalty?

This evolving situation is a reminder that pricing isn’t just about numbers — it’s about relationships and reputation in the luxury marketplace.
