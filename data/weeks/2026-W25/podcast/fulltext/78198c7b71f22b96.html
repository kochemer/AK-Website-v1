This Jewelry Retailer Skipped the Models and Let Its Own Employees Tell the Real Story of Motherhood

Day’s Jewelers built its Mother’s Day campaign around the unscripted voices of its employee-owners — and the authenticity paid off.

Published

2 days ago

on

June 19, 2026

By

Eileen McClelland Instore July 2026 Issue
  • Share
  • Tweet

Nikia Levesque-Meyer, VP of marketing, with her own mom for the campaign.

WHEN DAY’S JEWELERS BEGAN planning its 2026 Mother’s Day marketing campaign, the employee-owned Maine-based retailer didn’t have far to go to find talent.

Rather than hiring professional models or centering the campaign on product shots, the company built “The Enduring Bond” around the real stories of its own employee-owners: women navigating motherhood in all its complicated, joyful and emotional forms.

Day’s office manager Ashley, for example, marvels at her baby’s “tiny little giggle and her little red eyebrows” in a video filmed for the campaign. Another participant, Lauryl, payroll and benefits coordinator, equates being a grandmother of three with a rebirth “because these boys look just like my son.”

For independent jewelers wanting to connect authentically with customers, the campaign offers an example of how storytelling can create stronger emotional engagement than a traditional product-driven approach.

Day’s employee-owners (clockwise from left) — new mom Ashley, grandmom Lauryl, and dog mom Amelia — share their heartfelt stories.

“We wanted to feature different facets of motherhood,” says Nikia Levesque-Meyer, vice president of marketing for Day’s Jewelers. “Women who are trying to conceive. Pet moms. Grandmothers. First-time moms. Mothers with toddlers. We wanted to show what that journey looks like now — chaotic, beautiful, emotional and real.” Levesque-Meyer, who also participated in the campaign, touches on her own infertility in one scene of the video and then talks with her own mom about their relationship. “I hope to be half the mom she is someday,” she says.

The campaign appeared across multiple platforms, including YouTube, traditional television, social media and in-store digital displays. Cinematic video and portrait photography focused less on jewelry itself and more on the emotional connections behind it. Jewelry became a supporting character — a symbol woven naturally into life moments rather than the focal point.

The creative team developed directional scripts and knew what emotional territory each story needed to cover, but the words were entirely the employees’ own. “We didn’t do any coaching or rehearsal,” Levesque-Meyer says. Participants were asked questions including: “What has your motherhood journey been like?” and “What has been the most magical moment of becoming a mom?” The responses shaped the campaign organically.

Advertisement

Day’s has 130 employee-owners across eight locations in Maine and New Hampshire, but Levesque-Meyer says selecting participants felt like a natural process. “One of the greatest things about Day’s is our culture,” she says. “Even though we have multiple locations, we’re very connected to our people.” Employees embraced the project, which also strengthened camaraderie across the organization and opened the door for future employee-centered storytelling initiatives.

The campaign also demonstrated that emotional storytelling does not necessarily require massive production budgets. “Consumers respond well to seeing real people,” Levesque-Meyer says. “They notice when things don’t feel authentic.”

The jewelry styling itself was carefully tailored to each participant’s stage of motherhood. A pineapple charm bracelet symbolized hope and infertility awareness alongside a butterfly necklace representing transformation. A grandmother was styled in heirloom-inspired colored gemstone jewelry and lockets designed to evoke generational connection. A mother of a toddler wore classic diamond studs chosen for their simplicity and everyday wearability.

That storytelling translated into product performance.

Larkin, marketing and social media coordinator, with her daughter.

Charms and charm bracelets performed especially well during the campaign, along with Maine tourmaline and other colored gemstone jewelry, diamond bracelets and religious necklaces such as crosses and angel wings. Strong-performing brands included Ania Haie, Rembrandt Charms and Dee Berkley Jewelry, along with Day’s own in-house designs.

The company supported the campaign with in-store experiences, digital gift guides and video integration throughout its stores. Full campaign videos played on digital screens while signage reinforced the storytelling themes. Associates helped customers find pieces that felt deeply personal, from birthstone jewelry and charm bracelets to custom designs that reflected individual family stories.

For independent retailers, the takeaway may be that customers increasingly connect with brands willing to show vulnerability and humanity rather than perfection.

“This was an authentic way to show motherhood,” Levesque-Meyer says. “Motherhood is not perfect. It’s messy and hectic and overwhelming sometimes. It’s not all sunflowers and daisies and beautiful jewelry all the time. But beautiful jewelry helps.”
