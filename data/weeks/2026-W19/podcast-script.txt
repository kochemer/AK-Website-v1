[Segment 1: Amazon launches B2B supply chain service]

Host:  
First up, Amazon is making a significant push deeper into the B2B logistics market with the launch of Amazon Supply Chain Services, or ASCS, announced on May 5th, 2026. This new platform extends Amazon’s logistics capabilities beyond its traditional marketplace sellers, integrating freight, warehousing, fulfillment, and parcel delivery for a broad range of industries — retail, health care, manufacturing, you name it.

What’s notable here is that Amazon is not just offering last-mile delivery but a full-service supply chain solution. This means businesses can now tap into Amazon’s sophisticated freight network to move raw materials, store inventory, and deliver finished goods efficiently. It’s a big step in Amazon’s growth strategy, positioning itself as a logistics powerhouse that other companies can rely on rather than just a marketplace giant.

For luxury and jewellery brands, this could mean faster, more reliable supply chains, and more flexibility in inventory management — especially as consumer expectations for speed and transparency continue to rise. We’ll be watching closely how competitors respond to this expanded offering.

[Segment 2: Ecommerce Trends – How online retailers compete against Amazon]

Host:  
Following the headline-grabbing news that GameStop made a $56 billion bid to acquire eBay, CEO Ryan Cohen has outlined a clear vision: to position eBay as a serious competitor to Amazon in the ecommerce space.

This isn’t just a financial play. It’s about leveraging eBay’s massive user base, unique marketplace model, and combining it with GameStop’s retail reach and tech investments to create a more agile, customer-focused platform. Cohen’s strategy involves enhancing user experience, expanding product categories, and integrating better logistics — potentially even tapping into Amazon’s own supply chain innovations we just discussed.

For those of us tracking luxury ecommerce, this signals a potential shift. eBay, long known for auctions and collectibles, could become a stronger platform for luxury goods, jewellery, and high-end products, especially if it leverages authenticated sales and improved buyer protections.

Ultimately, this move underscores the intense battle for ecommerce supremacy and how retailers are exploring new strategies to carve out their niche against the Amazon behemoth.

[Segment 3: How Californian grocer Stater Bros. shines a light on the future of in-store retail media globally]

Host:  
Californian grocery chain Stater Bros. Markets is showcasing the future of in-store retail media by partnering with In-Store Marketplace, or ISM. Using ISM’s programmatic digital platform, Stater Bros. is integrating dynamic, data-driven media directly into its physical stores.

This approach allows for real-time, targeted advertising on digital screens throughout the store, tailored to shopper demographics, time of day, and even inventory levels. It’s a smart blend of digital and physical retail that enhances shopper engagement, boosts promotional effectiveness, and opens new revenue streams from brands looking to reach customers right at the point of purchase.

For luxury retailers and jewellery stores, the implications are significant. Imagine personalized messaging and dynamic displays that cater to affluent shoppers, highlighting exclusive collections or limited-time offers as they browse in-store. This kind of tech-driven retail media helps bridge the gap between ecommerce’s data richness and the sensory experience of brick-and-mortar luxury shopping.

It’s a clear example of how physical retail can evolve and remain relevant in a digital-first world.

[Segment 4: LVMH Australia increases dividend despite sales decline]

Host:  
Despite reporting a decline in sales recently, LVMH Australia has increased its dividend payments to the parent company by over 20%, according to a May 2026 report from Jeweller - Business News.

This move might seem counterintuitive at first glance, but it reflects LVMH’s confidence in its long-term strategy and cash flow management. Increasing dividends during a sales dip suggests the company is focusing on shareholder value and possibly reallocating resources to invest in growth markets or innovation.

For luxury investors and industry watchers, it signals that LVMH sees stability and resilience in the Australian luxury market, even amid short-term challenges. This could be due to strong brand loyalty, effective marketing, or the high-margin nature of luxury goods.

On the flip side, it also raises questions about how luxury groups balance sales performance with shareholder expectations — especially in a market facing economic pressures or shifting consumer behaviors.

[Segment 5: Jewellery stolen in shocking Sydney ram raid]

Host:  
In Sydney’s upscale Double Bay district, a jewellery store fell victim to a ram raid, where a stolen vehicle was driven into the storefront, enabling thieves to grab high-value jewellery before escaping.

This brazen crime highlights ongoing security challenges for jewellery retailers globally, especially those in high-end markets where the inventory is both valuable and relatively easy to steal.

It serves as a stark reminder for luxury jewellers to invest in advanced security measures — from reinforced storefronts and smart alarms to AI-powered surveillance and real-time monitoring.

With ecommerce growth, some brands are shifting focus to online sales to mitigate physical store risks, but the in-person luxury shopping experience remains vital. Finding the right balance between security and customer experience will be crucial going forward.

[Segment 6: Amazon Opens Up Its Logistics Network to Other Businesses in Growth Push]

Host:  
Amazon’s launch of Amazon Supply Chain Services is more than just a new product — it’s a strategic opening of its vast logistics network to third-party businesses.

According to Business of Fashion, this initiative allows companies to move, store, and deliver everything from raw materials to finished products using Amazon’s freight network. Essentially, Amazon is positioning itself as a one-stop logistics provider, competing not just with traditional carriers but also with third-party logistics firms.

This expansion is a double-edged sword for luxury and ecommerce players. On one hand, it offers access to Amazon’s unmatched scale and technology, potentially reducing costs and improving delivery times. On the other hand, it intensifies Amazon’s influence over the entire supply chain, raising concerns about dependency and competitive advantage.

For luxury brands wary of commoditization, navigating this new logistics landscape will require strategic partnerships and possibly exploring alternative logistics options to maintain brand control and exclusivity.
