[Segment 1: Payments, Fraud and AI: Why Retailers Can’t Afford to Ignore AML in 2026]

Host:  
First up, a crucial topic for online retailers — anti-money laundering, or AML. According to Retail TouchPoints, 2026 brings a new urgency for retailers to address AML head-on, especially as customer payment preferences continue to diversify. We’re no longer just talking credit cards and PayPal; bank transfers, e-wallets, buy-now-pay-later options, and even cryptocurrencies are becoming mainstream. While these options improve convenience and reach, they also open new avenues for fraud and financial crime.

AI is playing a dual role here. On one hand, advanced algorithms can help detect suspicious patterns far more effectively than traditional methods. On the other, fraudsters are increasingly sophisticated, sometimes leveraging AI themselves. Retailers ignoring AML risks do so at their peril — regulatory penalties, reputational damage, and financial losses are all looming threats. The takeaway? Integrating AI-driven AML solutions isn’t just a nice-to-have; it’s a business imperative as we move deeper into 2026.

[Segment 2: Best Buy Online Marketplace Gains Traction as New Profit Driver]

Host:  
Best Buy is making headlines once again — not for its core electronics sales, which have seen some unevenness, but for its growing online marketplace. Digital Commerce 360 reports that in fiscal Q4 2026, Best Buy’s marketplace generated around $300 million in gross merchandise value. That’s a serious contribution to the bottom line and signals a strategic shift.

Unlike direct sales, online marketplaces allow third-party sellers to offer products on Best Buy’s platform, expanding assortment without the inventory risk. For a retailer facing fluctuating demand and high costs, this model offers a valuable new revenue stream. It’s also a nod to the evolving ecommerce landscape, where even established brands are embracing the marketplace approach to stay competitive.

For luxury and jewellery brands watching closely, Best Buy’s success underscores the importance of diverse sales channels and leveraging digital platforms to capture incremental sales.

[Segment 5: Dior Builds a Dream Store. Kohl’s Builds a Deal Rack. Both Have a Lesson for Jewelers.]

A fascinating piece from Instore Magazine compares two very different retail strategies: Dior’s creation of an ultra-luxe “dream store” experience versus Kohl’s focus on value-driven deal racks. This dichotomy highlights the “barbell economy” — where consumers either gravitate towards premium experiences or hunt for bargains.

For jewellery retailers, the takeaway is clear: understanding your customer’s position on the luxury-value spectrum is essential. Some clients will seek exclusivity, craftsmanship, and immersive retail spaces like Dior’s. Others may prioritize affordability and deals, akin to Kohl’s approach.

Balancing these demands in 2026 might mean refining your brand’s retail environment or expanding your product mix to appeal to both ends of the market without diluting your identity.
