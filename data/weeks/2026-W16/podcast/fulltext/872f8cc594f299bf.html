First-quarter revenue at Kering drops 6pc

April 15, 2026

Gucci remained the group’s most pressing challenge, while Kering’s jewelry houses, including Boucheron, showed strong Q1 2026 performances. Image courtesy of Boucheron

 

By Amirah Keaton for Luxury Daily News Service

Amid an ongoing restructuring of its house portfolio, French luxury conglomerate Kering is sharing its latest quarterly report.

For the first quarter of 2026, company revenues remained stable on a comparable basis year-on-year, dipping 6 percent year-on-year on a reported basis, reaching 3.57 billion euros, or $4.21 billion at current exchange. Italian fashion label Gucci remained the group’s most pressing challenge, while Kering Eyewear and jewelry houses showed strong performances.

“This performance reflects the first tangible effects of our actions, despite a challenging geopolitical environment,” said Luca de Meo, CEO of Kering, in a statement.

“The first quarter of 2026 marked continued progress, as we executed with pace and focus,” Mr. de Meo said. “We have launched a Group platform designed to support the growth of our Houses and enhance efficiency, while also taking decisive steps across beauty, jewelry and real estate that have reinforced our balance sheet.

“I look forward to…Capital Markets Day, where we will present the Kering strategic roadmap: ReconKering.”

House by house
Sales at Gucci — it is Kering’s biggest brand by the numbers, and has been creatively helmed by ex-Balenciaga artistic director Demna Gvasalia since July of last year (see story) — fell 14 percent y-o-y in Q1, reported, garnering 1.35 billion euros, or about $1.59 billion at current exchange.

Kering’s Saint Laurent, Bottega Veneta, Balenciaga and Brioni brands each delivered year-on-year growth in the quarter.

A still from Gucci’s La Famiglia campaign, envisioned by current creative director Demna Gvasalia. Image courtesy of Gucci

Saint Laurent posted particularly strong results in shoes and ready-to-wear alongside the successful launch of the Mombasa handbag, while Bottega Veneta showed solid trends in Asia-Pacific. Balenciaga continued to grow on the strength of its City and Rodeo leather goods lines.

“Gucci remains our top priority,” Mr. de Meo said, in a statement.

“A comprehensive turnaround is underway, with decisive actions across client, distribution and, above all, the offer,” he said. “We have reset the product architecture and strengthened category focus, with new collections rolling out progressively in stores throughout the year.”

The conglomerate’s jewelry houses, on the other hand, were a standout, reaching a record $317.4 million in sales during the first three months of 2026, up 14 percent y-o-y on a reported basis.

Boucheron led the segment with the group’s strongest growth of the quarter, while Pomellato, DoDo and Qeelin each contributed meaningfully.

Kering Eyewear also reached a historic high at $577.1 million, rising 3 percent y-o-y, reported.
