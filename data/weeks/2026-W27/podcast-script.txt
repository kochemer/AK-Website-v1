As we consider AI’s growing influence in commerce, it’s important to address some of the challenges that come with digital transactions—especially fraud.

A new report from Chargebacks911 has revealed a worrying trend for retailers: more than 80% have experienced a rise in so-called “friendly fraud.” For those unfamiliar, friendly fraud occurs when customers dispute legitimate transactions with their credit card issuers, often resulting in chargebacks for merchants. Unlike classic fraud, where stolen cards are used, friendly fraud typically involves the actual cardholder, sometimes claiming they didn’t receive an item or didn’t authorize a purchase, even when they did. This form of fraud is particularly insidious because it’s difficult to detect and even harder to prevent using traditional tools. For luxury and ecommerce brands, the rise in friendly fraud can mean significant financial losses, increased scrutiny from payment processors, and a damaged relationship with customers. The report notes that as friendly fraud becomes more prevalent, merchants are turning to advanced AI technologies to help detect suspicious patterns, flag potentially fraudulent disputes, and even automate parts of the resolution process. For example, AI can analyze transaction histories, shipping data, and customer behavior to identify discrepancies that might indicate fraud. It can also help merchants respond more effectively to chargeback claims, providing documentation and evidence to support the legitimacy of the transaction. In the jewellery sector, where high-ticket items are often shipped with strict security, friendly fraud poses a unique challenge—customers may claim non-receipt or unauthorized purchases even when all protocols have been followed. The adoption of AI-powered fraud mitigation is not just a luxury but a necessity in today’s environment. The industry must continue to innovate, balancing seamless customer experiences with robust security measures. As AI tools become more sophisticated, we may see a shift in how disputes are handled, with technology taking a front seat in both prevention and resolution. It’s a reminder that as commerce evolves, so do the threats—and staying ahead requires constant vigilance and investment in the latest technology.

Moving from the challenges of fraud to the shifting patterns in retail, let’s talk about how the back-to-school season is being transformed by changes in the ecommerce calendar.

This year, Amazon’s decision to move Prime Day earlier in the summer has had a ripple effect across the retail landscape, especially when it comes to back-to-school shopping. Traditionally, the back-to-school period would kick off in late July or early August, but with Prime Day now landing in June, major retailers like Walmart and Target have followed suit, launching deals and targeting budget-conscious parents well in advance of the usual season. The result? Back-to-school has effectively become a summer-long event rather than a concentrated shopping window. This shift has several implications for luxury and mainstream retailers alike. First, it means that brands need to be ready earlier than ever before, with inventory, promotions, and marketing campaigns all aligned to capture consumer attention as soon as the season starts. For luxury brands and high-end retailers, there’s an opportunity to participate in the extended season by offering premium products—think designer backpacks, upscale stationery, or exclusive jewellery pieces designed for students and parents looking to celebrate milestones. The extension of the season also changes how shoppers approach their purchases. Instead of waiting for last-minute deals, consumers are spreading out their spending, making more considered choices, and looking for value over time. For ecommerce businesses, this provides a longer runway to engage customers, test different offers, and optimize their marketing strategies. However, it also means increased competition, as more retailers vie for attention over a longer period. The move underscores a broader trend in retail: the calendar is being rewritten by digital giants, and everyone else must adapt or risk being left behind. For the luxury sector, agility and the ability to anticipate and respond to these shifts will be critical in maintaining relevance and capturing share in an increasingly crowded and dynamic marketplace.

While extended shopping seasons offer new opportunities, technology is also reshaping how retailers interact with their customers—sometimes with mixed results.

AI chatbots have been heralded as a major step forward in retail customer service, promising instant responses and around-the-clock assistance. However, a recent report from Jeweller - Business News highlights that, particularly in the jewellery industry, these chatbots are still far from being a silver bullet. While they can handle basic inquiries and provide information about products or orders, many customers report feeling unsatisfied with the level of service provided by AI, especially when it comes to complex or emotionally-charged purchases. Jewellery is inherently personal, often tied to significant life events like engagements, anniversaries, or milestones. Customers expect not just accurate information, but empathy, expertise, and a sense of care—qualities that current chatbots struggle to deliver. The technology is improving quickly, with advances in natural language processing and contextual understanding, but we’re not quite at the point where AI can fully replace the human touch in luxury retail. This is particularly true when customers have detailed questions about gemstones, custom orders, or the provenance of unique pieces. For retailers, the challenge is to integrate AI in a way that enhances, rather than diminishes, the overall customer experience. Hybrid models, where chatbots handle routine queries and seamlessly escalate more complex issues to human associates, are becoming increasingly popular. The report suggests that while chatbots are an important tool in the modern jewellery retailer’s toolkit, they should be seen as an augmentation rather than a replacement for skilled human advisors. The takeaway for the luxury industry is clear: technology can enable better service, but it must be implemented thoughtfully, with a focus on maintaining the personal connections that define high-end retail.

Speaking of challenges in the jewellery sector, another troubling trend has emerged in Tasmania, where consumers are being warned about a rise in fraudulent online “ghost stores.”

According to a recent report by Jeweller - Business News, there has been a notable increase in these fake online shops targeting the local jewellery market. Ghost stores appear legitimate on the surface, often featuring professional-looking websites, attractive product photos, and enticing offers. However, once customers make a purchase, they either receive counterfeit items or, more commonly, nothing at all. This type of scam is particularly damaging in the jewellery industry, where trust and authenticity are everything. For consumers, the allure of discounted luxury items can be hard to resist, especially when the sites mimic the branding and language of reputable retailers. For legitimate jewellery businesses, the proliferation of ghost stores represents both a reputational and financial threat. Not only do these scams siphon off potential sales, but they also erode consumer confidence in online jewellery shopping as a whole. In response, authorities and industry associations in Tasmania are ramping up efforts to educate consumers about the risks, encouraging shoppers to verify the legitimacy of online retailers before making purchases. This includes looking for secure payment options, checking for clear contact information, and researching customer reviews. For the luxury and ecommerce sectors, the lesson is clear: as online sales channels expand, so do opportunities for bad actors. Combatting ghost stores will require ongoing vigilance, collaboration between industry and regulators, and the adoption of advanced verification technologies. While AI and automation can help flag suspicious sites and transactions, the human element—raising awareness and building trust—remains just as critical in the fight against online retail fraud.

On the subject of transformative shifts in retail, let’s turn our attention to the rapid evolution of quick commerce, especially as it relates to urban markets in India.

A new report from Economic Times - Technology highlights that quick commerce—where goods are delivered to consumers in as little as 10 to 30 minutes—is no longer viewed as a luxury service reserved for the affluent. Instead, it’s becoming a permanent structural shift in urban retail, with the market expected to reach an impressive $60 billion by fiscal year 2031. This growth is being driven by rising customer demand, increased order frequency, and a broader mix of non-grocery items being offered through these platforms. For urban consumers, the expectation of near-instant delivery has quickly transitioned from novelty to necessity, influencing not just how they shop, but what they shop for. Luxury and high-end brands are beginning to explore how quick commerce can fit into their strategies, whether it’s offering same-day delivery for exclusive products or leveraging quick commerce platforms to reach new customer segments. For the jewellery sector, the ability to deliver select pieces rapidly could be a game-changer, particularly for last-minute gifts or urgent purchases. However, the shift also presents operational and logistical challenges. Quick commerce requires robust supply chains, real-time inventory management, and the ability to fulfill orders with exceptional speed and accuracy. For retailers, especially those in the luxury space, maintaining the high standards of service and presentation expected by discerning customers is vital. The rise of quick commerce also signals broader changes in consumer behavior—convenience is king, and brands that can’t keep up risk being left behind. As the industry adapts, we can expect to see new partnerships, technology investments, and innovative fulfillment models designed to meet the demands of the modern urban shopper. This isn’t just a passing trend; it’s a fundamental shift that will shape the future of retail in some of the world’s fastest-growing markets.

As retail and commerce models evolve, it’s important to remember that not all sectors benefit equally from these shifts. The beauty industry, for example, is facing a unique set of challenges when it comes to scaling small brands.

According to a recent article in Fashionista, it’s becoming increasingly difficult for small beauty brands to achieve growth and success in the luxury consumer market. Rising production costs, stricter demands from retailers, and limited access to manufacturing resources are all creating significant barriers to scale. Unlike established players who can leverage economies of scale and long-standing relationships with suppliers, indie beauty brands often struggle to secure favorable terms, reliable production schedules, and shelf space with major retailers. At the same time, the cost of digital advertising—which was once seen as a great equalizer—has skyrocketed, making it harder for smaller brands to reach new customers and build awareness. For luxury beauty, these challenges are compounded by high consumer expectations for quality, innovation, and ethical sourcing. Retailers are also becoming more selective, often requiring extensive proof of performance, compliance, and sustainability before agreeing to stock new brands. This creates a catch-22 for small businesses: without retail distribution, it’s difficult to scale; without scale, it’s hard to secure distribution. The article notes that some brands are turning to direct-to-consumer models, pop-up experiences, and strategic partnerships as alternative growth strategies. However, even these approaches come with their own hurdles, from logistics to customer acquisition costs. The broader implication for the luxury sector is clear—while innovation and entrepreneurship remain vital, the road to success is getting steeper for small beauty brands. Supporting these companies, whether through industry partnerships, incubator programs, or investment in shared manufacturing resources, will be essential to preserving diversity and creativity in the luxury beauty market.
