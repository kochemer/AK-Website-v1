[Segment 1: Amazon expands AI-powered Shop Direct program]

Host:  
First up, Amazon is taking another big step in AI-powered retail with its expanded Shop Direct program. Originally launched to let customers discover products from other retailers, Amazon’s platform now uses AI to allow shoppers to actually buy items directly from those other retailers’ websites—even if those products aren’t sold on Amazon.com itself.

This is a significant evolution in ecommerce. Traditionally, Amazon has been the go-to place for buying products, but this expansion blurs the line between Amazon as a marketplace and Amazon as a discovery engine for the entire online retail ecosystem. Shoppers no longer have to switch sites; AI handles product discovery and purchase logistics seamlessly.

For luxury and jewellery brands, this could open new channels to reach customers who prefer to shop on Amazon but want access to exclusive or niche items not listed there. It also challenges retailers to integrate their inventories and checkout processes more tightly with Amazon’s AI systems. The convenience factor here is key—bringing more products and sellers closer to the consumer without the friction of multiple websites.

We’re watching this space closely because it could reshape how luxury ecommerce operates, especially as AI continues to personalize and streamline the shopping experience.

[Segment 2: Shopify’s purchases inside ChatGPT through agentic storefronts]

Host:  
Shopify is innovating at the intersection of AI and ecommerce with a new feature called “agentic storefronts.” This essentially lets merchants’ products become discoverable and purchasable directly within ChatGPT. Yes, you heard that right: You can chat with an AI bot and end up buying stuff from your favorite Shopify merchants without ever leaving the chat interface.

This development comes as OpenAI has stepped back from its Instant Checkout initiative, signaling a shift in how conversational commerce is evolving. Rather than OpenAI handling the full checkout flow, Shopify is empowering merchants to build storefronts that the AI can interact with intelligently, providing tailored product recommendations and completing purchases through a more integrated experience.

For luxury brands and jewellery retailers, the implications are huge. Imagine personalized, AI-guided shopping experiences where customers get expert-like assistance, product education, and seamless purchasing—all conversationally. It could reduce friction, increase conversion rates, and create a new standard for online luxury retail.

Plus, the agentic commerce model Shopify is promoting aligns perfectly with what we’re seeing in the industry: AI acting as a proactive agent rather than a passive tool, anticipating customer needs and driving sales in a deeply personalized manner.

[Segment 3: B2B and B2C companies increase AI investment as agentic commerce gains traction]

Host:  
A recent study commissioned by Logicbroker highlights a notable surge in AI investments across both B2B and B2C ecommerce companies. The goal? Enhancing customer experiences, optimizing pricing, improving inventory management, and streamlining fulfillment processes.

What stands out is the preparation for what’s being called “agentic commerce.” This is where AI agents proactively interact with customers, suppliers, and inventory systems to deliver a seamless end-to-end shopping journey. It’s not just about chatbots answering questions anymore; it’s about AI driving the commerce process autonomously.

For luxury and jewellery sectors, which often juggle complex inventory and high-touch customer service, this shift is particularly promising. AI can help anticipate demand, personalize offers, and even manage logistics to fulfill orders faster and more accurately.

The report suggests companies increasing AI budgets are positioning themselves to stay competitive in an environment where consumers expect immediacy, personalization, and flawless service. The rise of agentic commerce could redefine how brands engage with customers, making AI a core component rather than a supplementary tool.

[Segment 4: Advertising fraud causing billions in losses]

Host:  
A recent report has uncovered a sobering reality for the Australian economy—and particularly for the jewellery sector. Advertising fraud caused by ‘bot traffic’ is draining over five billion dollars annually.

Bots mimic human clicks and interactions, skewing analytics and draining advertising budgets without generating real customer engagement or sales. For jewellery retailers, where advertising budgets are critical for brand visibility and customer acquisition, this is a substantial loss.

The impact goes beyond just wasted money; it disrupts marketing strategies and undermines trust in digital advertising channels. Brands may end up chasing ineffective campaigns or overpaying for ads that never reach genuine buyers.

This problem highlights the need for better fraud detection technologies and more transparent advertising ecosystems, especially as luxury brands increasingly invest in digital marketing to reach affluent, discerning customers.
