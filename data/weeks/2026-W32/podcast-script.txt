Our first story is about Gorilla Commerce, a name that’s become synonymous with success on Amazon, now making a major leap into brick-and-mortar retail. Gorilla Commerce, the company behind those famous slip-resistant bath mats known as Gorilla Grip, has built a nine-figure business largely by mastering the Amazon marketplace. Their ascent is a case study in how a focused product line and sharp brand identity can cut through the noise of online retail. Now, they’re taking their expertise offline, launching Gorilla Grip products in over 2,600 Walmart stores across the United States. This expansion follows their entry into Walmart’s online marketplace two years ago, which evidently laid the groundwork for this physical retail move.

Why does this matter for the industry? For one, it’s a testament to the increasingly blurred lines between ecommerce and traditional retail. Gorilla Commerce’s journey underscores how brands that succeed online can leverage data, customer insights, and agile supply chains to capture shelf space in physical stores. Walmart’s decision to bring Gorilla Grip into thousands of locations is also telling. It suggests that big box retailers recognize the value and consumer demand generated by what were once online-only brands. This isn’t just a win for Gorilla Commerce; it’s a signal to other digital-first companies that omnichannel strategies aren’t just possible—they’re necessary for scaling up.

Moreover, Gorilla Commerce’s approach demonstrates the power of brand-building on platforms like Amazon. By establishing credibility, gathering reviews, and optimizing for search, they created a loyal customer base that now gives them a significant advantage as they transition into Walmart’s aisles. For the broader industry, this move could inspire a new wave of Amazon-native brands to think bigger—beyond the confines of digital marketplaces and into the world of mass retail. It’s a fascinating evolution, and one that highlights the importance of being nimble, data-driven, and customer-focused in today’s retail environment.

Of course, there are challenges ahead, especially around privacy, data security, and algorithmic transparency. Speaking of the future of shopping, let’s turn our attention to a platform that’s riding the live commerce wave all the way to a staggering $20 billion valuation.

Whatnot, a live shopping platform you may have heard about, has recently hit a $20 billion valuation, underscoring the explosive growth of live commerce. For those unfamiliar, Whatnot enables real-time buying and selling through livestreams—think QVC for the TikTok generation, but with a much broader range of products and a distinctly community-driven feel. The platform has capitalized on the surging interest in interactive, social shopping experiences, particularly among Gen Z and millennial consumers.

Why does this matter? First, it highlights a fundamental shift in how people want to discover and purchase products. Instead of static product pages, consumers are gravitating towards engaging, real-time content where they can interact with sellers, ask questions, and even participate in auctions. This not only drives urgency and excitement, but also builds trust—buyers get to see products up close and in action before making a purchase. For sellers, live shopping opens up new ways to build community, foster loyalty, and differentiate their offerings in an increasingly crowded ecommerce landscape.

Whatnot’s meteoric rise also reflects broader trends in digital commerce. As traditional online shopping experiences become commoditized, platforms that offer entertainment, community, and authentic engagement are standing out. The $20 billion valuation sends a clear message to investors and retailers alike: live commerce isn’t a fad, it’s a fundamental evolution in how we buy and sell online. Major brands are already experimenting with their own livestream shopping initiatives, and we can expect to see even more innovation—and competition—in this space.

The challenge, as always, will be sustaining growth and ensuring that live commerce remains accessible and appealing to a wide audience. There’s also the question of monetization: how will platforms balance the needs of creators, brands, and consumers while building sustainable business models? For now, though, Whatnot’s success is a powerful indicator that the future of ecommerce will be as much about entertainment and community as it is about convenience and choice.

As we reflect on the intersection of sports, fashion, and luxury, let’s turn to another industry grappling with the challenge of keeping customers engaged—streaming video on demand.

The streaming industry, or SVOD (subscription video on demand), is entering a new phase where the battle isn’t just for content, but for customer relationships. According to a recent Forrester Blog, companies like Netflix are facing declining viewership between hit shows, raising serious questions about how to maintain engagement and loyalty in an increasingly crowded market.

In the early days of streaming, the formula seemed simple: produce must-watch content, attract subscribers, and watch the numbers grow. But as competition intensifies and the novelty of streaming wears off, platforms are realizing that hit shows alone aren’t enough. Viewers are becoming more selective, hopping between services based on what’s trending, and often churning out once their favorite series ends. This has forced the industry to think more deeply about customer experience and long-term engagement.

What does this mean for the future of streaming? We’re likely to see a greater focus on personalization, community-building, and value-added services. Platforms might experiment with loyalty programs, interactive features, or even exclusive experiences designed to keep users coming back. The goal is to move beyond transactional relationships and create genuine connections with viewers. For brands and advertisers, this presents new opportunities to engage audiences in meaningful ways, but it also raises the bar for what it takes to stand out.

The challenge, of course, is balancing content investment with customer retention strategies. As the market matures, streaming services will need to innovate not just in what they offer, but in how they foster lasting relationships. The winners in this next phase will be those who can turn fleeting viewership into enduring loyalty.
